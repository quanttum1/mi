Does social media cause political polarisation?

I've been working on my LC Politics & Society research project recently. The question I focused on was basically: is the way modern media works actually pushing people further apart politically? At first, it kind of feels obvious that the answer is yes. We always hear about echo chambers, biased feeds, outrage, all that stuff. And there's definitely truth to it.

A lot of what I found shows that social media algorithms tend to feed us content we already agree with. So over time, your feed becomes more and more one-sided. That's where the idea of "echo chambers" comes from. On top of that, the kind of posts that get the most attention are usually the emotional ones: they cause anger, outrage, fear, which doesn't help calm, balanced discussion.

But here's the part that surprised me: even when people are shown opposing views, it doesn't necessarily make them more open-minded. In some studies, it actually made people double down on their original opinions. Instead of thinking "maybe I'm wrong," people tend to argue back in their heads and become even more convinced they're right.

There were also some really interesting experiments where people stopped using platforms like Facebook and Instagram for a few weeks. You'd probably expect that to reduce polarisation. But the results were kind of mixed. People who took a break were less politically active and believed less misinformation, which may sound positive, but they were also less informed about current events. And overall, their core political views didn't really change that much.

Even more surprising, in one study from a very divided society, people who stayed on social media actually had slightly better attitudes towards other groups than those who left. That suggests social media can sometimes expose people to different perspectives in a way they wouldn't normally experience offline.

So the main thing I've taken away is that it's not as simple as "social media = bad". It definitely can increase division, especially because of algorithms and emotional content. But it's not the only factor, and in some cases it can even have the opposite effect.

I guess the best way to think about it is that social media is just a really powerful tool. It can push people apart or bring them closer together depending on how it's used, what content is being shared, and the kind of environment people are in.
